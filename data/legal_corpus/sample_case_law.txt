SMITH v. JONES CORPORATION
Supreme Court of California
Case No. 2023-CA-001234

FACTS:
Plaintiff John Smith entered into an employment agreement with Defendant Jones Corporation on January 15, 2022. The agreement contained a non-compete clause prohibiting Smith from working for competitors within a 50-mile radius for two years after termination.

Smith was terminated on March 1, 2023, and subsequently accepted employment with TechCorp, a competitor located 30 miles from Jones Corporation's headquarters. Jones Corporation filed suit seeking injunctive relief and damages.

ISSUE:
Whether the non-compete clause in the employment agreement is enforceable under California law.

HOLDING:
The Court held that the non-compete clause is unenforceable as it violates California Business and Professions Code Section 16600, which prohibits contracts that restrain trade.

REASONING:
California has a strong public policy against non-compete agreements. Section 16600 provides that "every contract by which anyone is restrained from engaging in a lawful profession, trade, or business of any kind is to that extent void."

The Court found that the geographic restriction of 50 miles and the two-year duration were unreasonable restraints on Smith's ability to earn a livelihood. The restriction was not narrowly tailored to protect legitimate business interests.

CONCLUSION:
The non-compete clause is void and unenforceable. Jones Corporation's motion for injunctive relief is denied. Smith is free to pursue employment with TechCorp without restriction.

DISSENT:
Justice Martinez dissented, arguing that the non-compete clause was reasonable and necessary to protect Jones Corporation's trade secrets and customer relationships.
