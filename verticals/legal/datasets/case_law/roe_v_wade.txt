ROE v. WADE
Supreme Court of the United States
410 U.S. 113 (1973)

JUSTICE BLACKMUN delivered the opinion of the Court.

FACTS:
Jane Roe, a pregnant single woman, brought a class action challenging the constitutionality of the Texas criminal abortion laws, which proscribe procuring or attempting an abortion except on medical advice for the purpose of saving the mother's life.

The case was brought against Henry Wade, the District Attorney of Dallas County, Texas, who was responsible for enforcing the abortion laws.

ISSUE:
Whether the Texas criminal abortion statutes, which prohibit abortions except to save the life of the mother, violate the Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment.

HOLDING:
The Supreme Court held that the Texas criminal abortion statutes are unconstitutional and that a woman has a constitutional right to choose to have an abortion.

REASONING:
The Court recognized that the right to privacy is broad enough to encompass a woman's decision whether or not to terminate her pregnancy. This right is not absolute, however, and must be considered against important state interests in regulation.

The Court established a trimester framework:
- First trimester: The abortion decision must be left to the woman and her attending physician.
- Second trimester: The state may regulate abortion procedures in ways reasonably related to maternal health.
- Third trimester: The state may regulate or even proscribe abortion except where necessary to preserve the life or health of the mother.

CONCLUSION:
The Court held that the Texas statutes are unconstitutional and enjoined their enforcement.

SIGNIFICANCE:
This landmark decision established a woman's constitutional right to abortion and created the framework for abortion regulation that remained in place until it was overturned in Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization (2022).

The decision was highly controversial and remains one of the most debated Supreme Court cases in American history.
